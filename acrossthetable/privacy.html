Privacy Policy

Across the Table · last updated 9 October 2026

Across the Table collects no data about you or anyone you play with. It has no account and no sign-in, and the app makes no connection to the internet. Nothing you do in it leaves your iPhone.

What the app stores

On your iPhone only, two things: the help setting (who gets help, and how much) and whether sound is on. Nothing else: no names, no identifier, no scores and no record of the games you played.

This lives inside the app's own storage. Deleting the app deletes it. Like other app data it may be included in an iPhone backup you make yourself, to your own computer or your own iCloud account. That copy is yours and is not visible to me.

What the app does not do

  • No analytics, telemetry or usage reporting.
  • No advertising and no advertising identifier.
  • No tracking, and no data shared with anyone for any purpose.
  • No third-party software development kits of any kind.
  • No account, no email address, no name, no sign-in.
  • No connection to the internet of its own. It plays the same in airplane mode.
  • No access to your location, microphone, camera, photos, contacts, calendar, health data or files.
  • No notifications.
  • No chat, no leaderboards and no way to reach or be reached by anyone. The two players share one phone at one table.

Your age band, where a law asks for it

A few places now require every app to find out which age band the person using it is in. Texas has since June 2026, and other places are following. Where such a law covers you, Across the Table asks your iPhone for your age band when it opens: "under 13", for example, or "18 and over". It is a band and never a birthday. Your iPhone may show you a sheet first, and for a child's account a parent or guardian may already have decided what apps are told.

Across the Table then does nothing with the answer. The games are the same at every age, so there is nothing in it to turn on or off. The help setting is chosen by the players at the table and has nothing to do with it. The answer is not saved, not sent anywhere and never seen by me. Outside those places, the app does not ask at all.

The age band comes from Apple's Declared Age Range feature, which is part of the iPhone and works under Apple's privacy policy. To answer, the iPhone may check with Apple; the app takes no part in that. You can see and change what apps are told in the Settings app: your name, then Personal Information, then Age Range for Apps.

The two links in Help and privacy

The app's Help and privacy screen links to this page and the support page, which open in your web browser. That is the only thing in the app that leads out of it. These are plain pages: no cookies, no analytics, no scripts. They are hosted by GitHub Pages, which, like any web host, sees the address of a device that asks for a page. That is covered by GitHub's privacy statement, and none of it reaches me.

Purchases

Across the Table is bought once, from the App Store. The purchase is handled entirely by Apple. I never see your payment details, and the app itself stores nothing about the transaction.

What Apple may show me

If you have chosen in your iPhone's settings to share analytics and crash reports with app developers, Apple may show me aggregate, anonymized statistics: how many people downloaded the app, and crash reports with no identity attached. That is Apple's mechanism, it is off unless you turned it on and the app plays no part in it. It does not identify you.

Children

Across the Table is made for a grown-up and a child to play together. It collects nothing from anyone, which includes children. There is nothing in it that could identify a child, nothing that asks a child for anything and nobody in it to talk to. Where a law requires the age band described above, the app receives it and keeps none of it.

Your rights

Rights to access, correct, export or delete personal data, under Canadian law, the GDPR or anywhere else, apply to personal data that has been collected. None has been. There is no copy of anything about you to send you or to delete, and deleting the app removes everything it ever wrote.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of the app ever does something this page does not describe, this page changes first and the date at the top changes with it.
