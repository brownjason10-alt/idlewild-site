Privacy Policy

Sunday Call · last updated 8 October 2026

Sunday Call collects no data about you or your family. It has no account and no sign-in, there is no server behind it and the app makes no connection to the internet. What you write in it stays on your iPhone.

What the app keeps on your iPhone

Only what you put in it:

  • The people you add: a name, a phone number if you give one, which group they are in (your kids, family, grandchildren or friends), how often you usually reach out and how things are with them right now.
  • The contact you log with them: the day, the time if there is one, whether it was a call, a text, a video call or a visit, who started it, how long a call lasted and any short note you add.
  • What is coming up for them, and the things you've written down as worth remembering.
  • Your settings, including your reminder time and the two privacy switches for the widgets.

This sits in one file in the app's own storage on the phone, which its widgets read from too. Before the first change on any day, the app sets aside a copy of that file as it stood, so your family can be got back if the file is ever misread. It keeps the last three of these copies and none for longer than two weeks. If reminders are on, it also keeps the times reminders were due (never what they were about) so it can hold to three a week.

Deleting the app deletes all of it. Like other app data it may be included in an iPhone backup you make yourself, to your own computer or your own iCloud account. That copy is yours and is not visible to me.

Calls, texts and Contacts

  • Choose from Contacts opens iPhone's own contact list outside the app. Sunday Call receives only the name and the one number you tap, so it never asks for access to your contacts and never sees the rest.
  • Call hands the number to iPhone, which places the call. While you are on it, iPhone tells the app only that a call connected and when it ended, never who it was with or the number. That is how a call gets its length.
  • Text opens iPhone's message sheet with the number filled in. You write and send the message. The app is told only whether a message was sent, never what it said.
  • Calls and texts that come to you, and anything you do in the Phone or Messages apps, cannot be seen by any app. iPhone keeps them private. You log those yourself, or tap the tick once you've answered.

Widgets and the Lock Screen

Anyone who picks up your phone can see the Lock Screen, so by default the Lock Screen widgets show initials, not names. You can turn full names on in the app's At a glance tab. Your notes never appear on the Lock Screen. On the Home Screen, what to ask someone about shows only as a count unless you turn on the second switch there.

Reminders

Both kinds of reminder are off until you turn them on, and iPhone asks your permission first. They are scheduled on the phone, not sent from anywhere. A reminder never shows what you wrote, and it shows a name only if full names on the Lock Screen are on. There are never more than three in a week.

Siri and Shortcuts

"I talked to someone in Sunday Call" and the app's Shortcuts actions run on the phone and write to the same file. The one Siri offers by voice needs the phone to be unlocked, so it can't list your people to someone else. How Siri hears and understands what you say is part of iPhone and works under Apple's privacy policy.

What the app does not do

  • No analytics, telemetry or usage reporting.
  • No advertising and no advertising identifier.
  • No tracking, and no data shared with anyone for any purpose.
  • No third-party software development kits of any kind.
  • No account, no email address, no sign-in.
  • No connection to the internet of its own. It works the same in airplane mode (except, of course, for placing calls and sending texts).
  • No access to your location, microphone, camera, photos, calendar, health data or files, and no access to your contacts beyond the one you pick.
  • It never calls, texts or contacts anyone by itself. Every call and text starts with your tap.
  • Nothing for your family to install, and nothing is ever sent to them.

The two links in At a glance

At the bottom of the At a glance tab are Help and Privacy. They open the support page and this page in your web browser. These are plain pages: no cookies, no analytics, no scripts. They are hosted by GitHub Pages, which, like any web host, sees the address of a device that asks for a page. That is covered by GitHub's privacy statement, and none of it reaches me.

Purchases

Anything bought for Sunday Call is bought from the App Store and handled entirely by Apple. I never see your payment details.

What Apple may show me

If you have chosen in your iPhone's settings to share analytics and crash reports with app developers, Apple may show me aggregate, anonymized statistics: how many people downloaded the app, and crash reports with no identity attached. That is Apple's mechanism, it is off unless you turned it on and the app plays no part in it. It does not identify you, and it never includes anything you wrote.

Children

Sunday Call is made for parents. It collects nothing from anyone, which includes children and the grown kids you add to it. The people in it are never contacted by the app and never told they are in it.

Your rights

Rights to access, correct, export or delete personal data, under Canadian law, the GDPR or anywhere else, apply to personal data that has been collected. None has been. There is no copy of anything about you on any server to send you or to delete. Removing a person in the app deletes everything about them, and deleting the app removes everything it ever wrote.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of the app ever does something this page does not describe, this page changes first and the date at the top changes with it.
