Privacy Policy

Paint Shed · last updated 9 October 2026

Paint Shed doesn't collect any information. Your progress is saved on this iPhone and in its backups. No accounts, ads or tracking.

What the app stores

On your iPhone only: which puzzles you have solved and the stars you earned, the puzzle you were last on in each room, the work on a puzzle you left half-finished, which how-to cards you have already seen, and whether sound is on. Nothing else: no name, no identifier and no record of when or how long you played.

This lives inside the app's own storage. Erase progress in Settings clears it, and deleting the app deletes it. Like other app data it may be included in an iPhone backup you make yourself, to your own computer or your own iCloud account. That copy is yours and is not visible to me.

What the app does not do

  • No analytics, telemetry or usage reporting.
  • No advertising and no advertising identifier.
  • No tracking, and no data shared with anyone for any purpose.
  • No third-party software development kits of any kind.
  • No account, no email address, no name, no sign-in.
  • No Game Center, leaderboards or chat, and no way to reach or be reached by anyone.
  • No connection to the internet of its own. It plays the same in airplane mode.
  • No access to your location, microphone, camera, photos, contacts, calendar, health data or files.
  • No notifications.

Your age band, where a law asks for it

A few places now require every app to find out which age band the person using it is in. Texas has since June 2026, and other places are following. Where such a law covers you, Paint Shed asks your iPhone for your age band when it starts: "under 13", for example, or "18 and over". It is a band and never a birthday. Your iPhone may show you a sheet first, and for a child's account a parent or guardian may already have decided what apps are told.

Paint Shed then does nothing with the answer. The puzzles are the same at every age, so there is nothing in it to turn on or off. The answer is not saved, not sent anywhere and never seen by me. Outside those places, the app does not ask at all.

The age band comes from Apple's Declared Age Range feature, which is part of the iPhone and works under Apple's privacy policy. To answer, the iPhone may check with Apple; the app takes no part in that. You can see and change what apps are told in the Settings app: your name, then Personal Information, then Age Range for Apps.

The two links in Settings

The app's Settings has Help and Privacy policy, which open the support page and this page in your web browser. They are the only things in the app that lead out of it. These are plain pages: no cookies, no analytics, no scripts. They are hosted by GitHub Pages, which, like any web host, sees the address of a device that asks for a page. That is covered by GitHub's privacy statement, and none of it reaches me.

Purchases

Paint Shed is bought once, from the App Store. The purchase is handled entirely by Apple. I never see your payment details, and the app itself stores nothing about the transaction.

What Apple may show me

Apple reports to every developer how many copies of an app were downloaded and sold, by country, with nothing that identifies who bought them. If you have also chosen in your iPhone's settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple may show me aggregate, anonymized usage statistics and crash reports with no identity attached. That part is off unless you turned it on. Both are Apple's mechanisms; the app plays no part in them.

Once you have finished a room, the app may ask iOS to show Apple's usual "rate this app" prompt. iOS decides whether it appears, and any rating or review you leave goes to Apple under Apple's terms.

Children

Paint Shed collects nothing from anyone, which includes children. There is nothing in it that could identify a child and nobody in it to talk to. The app itself asks a child for nothing; the iPhone may ask about sharing an age band, as described above. Where a law requires the age band described above, the app receives it and keeps none of it.

Your rights

Rights to access, correct, export or delete personal data, under Canadian law, the GDPR or anywhere else, apply to personal data that has been collected. None has been. There is no copy of anything about you to send you or to delete, and deleting the app removes everything it ever wrote, apart from any copy in a backup of your own.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of the app ever does something this page does not describe, this page changes first and the date at the top changes with it.
